Observability & Evaluation

The Boring Infrastructure Behind Reliable AI Systems

By Tharun Kumar ReddySep 20265 min read

Nobody gets excited about tracing spans or eval harnesses. Everyone gets excited about the demo where the agent answers a hard question correctly on the first try. The gap between those two things is where most AI projects actually die — quietly, in production, a few weeks after the demo, once the questions stop being the ones in the slide deck.

Evaluation is not a one-time gate

Faithfulness, retrieval recall and precision, citation accuracy — metrics in the style of Ragas or DeepEval — are usually run once, right before launch, to satisfy a checklist. They're far more useful run continuously, against real traffic, because the failure modes that show up after launch are rarely the ones anyone tested for.

Observability has to span the whole stack

Tracing with OpenTelemetry and LLM-specific tooling like Langfuse or MLflow only pays off when it's tied back to data quality and cost, not treated as a separate dashboard for "the AI part." The systems that actually stay healthy correlate all three — data quality, generation quality, and cost — into one view instead of three dashboards nobody cross-references.

If you can't tell me why the model said what it said, you don't have an AI system — you have a slot machine with good PR.

This is a data engineering problem wearing an AI costume

Schema validation, testing, lineage, capacity planning — none of this is new. It's the same discipline data engineering has always required, just pointed at a new kind of pipeline where the transformation step happens to be a language model instead of a SQL query.

Why this is the part I want to own

It's not the flashiest layer of the stack, but it's the layer that decides whether an AI system survives contact with real usage instead of quietly rotting after the demo. That's the direction I'm deliberately building toward.
